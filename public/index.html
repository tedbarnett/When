Starting in New York. New Orleans next.

When launches local-first: a handful of genuinely great, hand-curated NYC calendars with live feeds, before anything else. New Orleans follows. Anywhere else, the code is open: run When for your own town.

Events are trapped in silos.

What's happening tonight in your city is scattered across Meetup, Facebook, Eventbrite, venue websites, and a hundred Instagram stories. Each one is a closed destination that holds listings hostage to its own growth. None of them answer the simple question: what's going on around here?

When is the open alternative. The unit isn't an app or an account, it's a calendar with an owner. A person who knows every jazz room in New York publishes a calendar, and you subscribe to it the way you'd subscribe to a newsletter. It shows up in the calendar app you already use, and it stays current.

Feeds are free forever. Reading is free. Publishing is free. Subscribing your calendar app is free. Data out is sacred: every page carries open standards, so nothing you build here is ever locked in.

The platform takes a small cut only when money moves: paid calendar subscriptions, tickets, tips. That's the whole business, and that's the .org promise. Think of it as Substack for calendars: own a calendar the way you own a newsletter.

Every calendar is four things at once

Publish once. It's instantly useful everywhere, with no exporting, no re-posting, no lock-in.

The people who make the scene should make money.

Curators

Offer a paid tier on your calendar: premium picks, early access, the weekly digest. You keep 90%. Nobody else pays taste-makers for knowing their city.

Hosts

Sell tickets and paid RSVPs natively for about a 1% platform fee plus card processing. The big ticketing platforms charge six times that. On free events, guests can tip you.

Venues

Embed your own calendar on your website free, and claim your venue page so every listing points home. Pro tools for branding and analytics come later.

When only earns when you do: a small share of payments, never a paywall on the feed itself.
